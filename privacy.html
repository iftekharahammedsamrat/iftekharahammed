2. What this website collects

This version of the website has no contact form, account area or checkout. It does not intentionally store personal information in the website itself.

WhatsApp enquiries

If you choose a WhatsApp button, you leave this website and open WhatsApp. The information processed is whatever you decide to send, such as your name, company, contact details and questions. It is used to reply to your enquiry and, where requested, discuss a possible service.

Technical logs

When the site is placed on a hosting service, that provider may create ordinary security and access logs containing an IP address, browser information, requested page and time. The final host and its retention settings must be documented before public launch.

Cookies and analytics

No advertising tracker, analytics service or non-essential cookie is configured in this build. If measurement tools are added later, this notice and any required consent controls must be updated first.

3. Demo media and external services

The demo video and fonts are served from this website and do not autoplay. Clicking WhatsApp transfers you to a service operated by Meta. WhatsApp then processes information under its own privacy terms.

Links to research sources or official guidance take you to the named third-party website. Those sites control their own logs, cookies and privacy practices.

4. How enquiry information is used

  • To answer the question or demo request you send.
  • To understand whether the service could fit your firm.
  • To prepare a proposal only where you ask for one.
  • To protect the enquiry channel from misuse or fraud.

Enquiry information should not be sold or used to send unrelated bulk marketing. It should be kept only while it is needed for the conversation, any requested follow-up, legal record-keeping or an active service relationship.

5. Voice-system data is agreed before launch

This website privacy notice is not a hidden authorisation to process your customers’ calls. A client-specific data schedule must be agreed before a receptionist is connected to real callers.

That schedule should state, in plain language:

  • whether calls are recorded and how callers are informed;
  • which contact, transcript, job and booking fields are stored;
  • which configured processors are involved, such as telephony, voice, calendar, database and automation providers;
  • where the information is processed and any international-transfer safeguards;
  • who can access it, how access is protected and how incidents are handled;
  • the exact retention period for audio, transcripts and customer records;
  • how corrections, exports and deletion requests are completed; and
  • the client’s and provider’s respective data-protection roles.

6. Your choices and rights

Depending on the applicable law and context, you may be able to ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection or a portable copy of personal information. You may also withdraw consent where consent was the basis used.

UK individuals can learn more or raise a concern with the Information Commissioner’s Office. Contacting Iftekhar first may allow a straightforward issue to be resolved quickly.
