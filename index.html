The job-site reality

You cannot hold a wrench and answer every incoming call.

The problem is not effort. One person cannot safely work on a boiler, drive between appointments and give every caller a proper first conversation at the same time.

01

On a job

You are under a sink or talking to a customer. The phone rings, then voicemail takes over.

02

Between jobs

You should not answer while driving. By the time you call back, the job may be elsewhere.

03

After hours

A genuine caller needs an answer, but you also need a working day that actually ends.

Boundaries create trust

A good receptionist does not turn every call into a booking.

Plumbing and heating calls are not all equal. The system needs firm limits for safety, suitability, pricing and capacity—and a clear route when it does not know.

  • Only configured services can enter the booking flow.
  • Charges are disclosed before the appointment is accepted.
  • Safety triggers interrupt the ordinary sales and booking route.
  • Unknown requests are captured or escalated—not answered with an invention.

The gas example is configured demo behaviour. It does not replace official emergency guidance or a qualified professional.

Practical questions

The concerns you should raise before trusting it.

A clear boundary is more useful than a vague promise that the AI can handle everything.

Hear the working demo
“My customers want to speak to me.”

The receptionist handles the first conversation and sends you the right details. You decide how your firm follows up and which calls should still reach a person.

“What if it sounds robotic?”

Do not take a copywriter’s word for it. Hear the UK-English demo, then test the configured version with the phrases and scenarios your customers actually use.

“What if it books the wrong thing?”

Services, prices, postcode areas, availability and booking windows are explicit rules. The system is not instructed to turn every call into an appointment.

“What happens on a gas-emergency call?”

A configured safety trigger stops the normal booking route and follows the agreed emergency flow. The demo proves the boundary; it does not replace official emergency guidance.

“I am already busy enough.”

The goal is not to fill the diary indiscriminately. It is to protect suitable calls, qualify them properly and reduce the repetitive administration around work you already receive.

“I already use WhatsApp.”

Keep WhatsApp for photos, updates and follow-up. The receptionist covers the incoming calls you cannot answer and sends the useful details into the agreed workflow.

“I do not want another system to manage.”

The service is configured with you, connected to the agreed diary and tested before launch. The management process and support boundaries are written into the pilot.

“Why should I trust a new provider?”

Start with evidence, not a promise: a real founder, a working fictional-company demo, visible process, documented limits and no invented testimonials or client results.

“How do I judge whether it costs too much?”

Compare the transparent monthly cost with your own gross profit from one suitable job and the phone administration it removes. That is a value check—not a guaranteed return.

“Why is there no cheap headline price?”

A fair price depends on call volume, included usage, setup and support. Those numbers should be explicit—not hidden behind a low entry price that does not cover the workflow shown here.
